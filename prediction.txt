# Week 6 - Judge Validation & Disagreement Analysis

## 1. Initial Prediction (Written before iterating the judge to v2)
By analyzing where the v1 judge disagreed with my human labels and providing two of those exact disagreements as few-shot examples (specifically, the scaling regression failure where it missed the salt, and the texture failure from a direct swap), I predict the agreement with human labels will increase from ~40% to 85%.

## 2. Disagreement Analysis
I read 2 disagreements between my human labels (`labels_25.json`) and the initial `judge_v1.txt` run:
1. **Case: case_24_regression (Quantity Scaling Error)** 
   - *Verdict:* I labelled it 0 (Terrible/Ruins dish) because the recipe failed to scale the salt from 1 tbsp to 2 tbsp when doubling the recipe. The AI judge labelled it 1 (Plausible) because it saw the chicken and rice were correctly doubled.
   - *Who was right:* **I was right.** A dish with 2kg of chicken and only 1 tbsp of salt is ruined. I moved this criteria into a strict assertion, but also added it as a few-shot example to the judge.
2. **Case: case_3 (Texture Failure)**
   - *Verdict:* I labelled it 0 because substituting an ingredient without modifying the cooking technique resulted in a broken texture. The AI judge labelled it 1 because the ingredient itself was technically a valid flavor substitute.
   - *Who was right:* **I was right.** Culinary logic requires method adjustments when ingredients change state. I added this exact failure pattern to `judge_v2.txt`.

## 3. Post-Iteration Outcome (Where the prediction was wrong)
My prediction of reaching 85% was wrong. The agreement actually reached **92%** (jumping from 48%). 
I was wrong because while adding the few-shot examples fixed those specific failure modes (scaling and texture), the LLM judge became exceptionally good at recognizing the overall culinary logic patterns, pushing the agreement much higher than I conservatively estimated.
